Full Layer / Full Pallet refers to ordering in complete, standardized units as they come off the production line and onto a warehouse pallet. 
A pallet is stacked in "layers" — each layer is a uniform grid of cases. 
For example, a pallet of 12-pk Cola might be 5 layers of 10 cases each (50 cases total). 
A "full pallet" order means the buyer orders in multiples of 50; a "full layer" order means multiples of 10.
Manufacturers and distributors strongly prefer full layer/pallet orders because they ship and store efficiently — no repackaging, no partial unstacking, minimal warehouse labor.

Broken Layer / Broken Pallet is when a customer orders a quantity that doesn't align to those clean multiples. 
If the layer holds 10 cases and someone orders 7, a warehouse worker has to physically break open a layer, pull 3 cases off, and handle the remainder. 
This triggers several real costs: additional pick-and-pack labor, increased damage risk, slower fulfillment, and wasted pallet space during transport.

In B2B order management, this surfaces as an exception because many CPG companies enforce minimum order quantities (MOQs) or layer/pallet rounding rules. 
When an inbound PO violates those rules, the system flags it. 
The typical resolution paths are: round the order up to the next full layer (with buyer approval), apply a broken case surcharge, reject the line and request a corrected PO, or grant an exception if the customer has negotiated that flexibility.
